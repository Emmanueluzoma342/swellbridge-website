The problem

Landberry and GLI Farms sell farmland and plantation investments. Many Nigerians have seen farm schemes go wrong, so an ad asking a stranger for money gets ignored.

What I did

I led with trust instead of a sales pitch. The ads invited people to a free webinar, where they could meet the people behind the business before being asked for anything.

I ran separate campaigns for separate groups: new realtors, experienced realtors, network marketers, land buyers, and parents for a children's offer. Everyone who signed up joined a WhatsApp group, where the offer was explained over several days rather than in one ad.

What happened

Across the campaigns in the screenshots, 4,579 people signed up, from ₦2.1M in ad spend. That includes the realtor campaigns shown in the next project.

The best campaign for complete strangers brought in 372 people at ₦197.62 each. The cheapest webinar sign-ups cost ₦170.59 each. The biggest single campaign brought in 1,742 people.

Three WhatsApp communities grew to 987 members. I also combined 22 separate lead files into one clean list of 3,744 unique contacts.

The proof

Getting people to sign up was my job, and the screenshots prove it. Turning sign-ups into sales was done by the client's own sales team, so I don't claim a sales figure here.

The problem

The developer was selling waterfront plots to two groups at once: buyers in Lagos, and Nigerians living abroad.

These two want different things. A buyer abroad is mostly afraid of being scammed from far away. A buyer in Lagos cares more about the location, the roads and the price. One ad can't speak to both.

What I did

I built two completely separate paths: different ads, different sign-up pages, different free guides and different sales pages.

For buyers abroad, everything led with proof: documented title, verification, and inspection they could do from a distance. For Lagos buyers, it led with the location and the entry price.

Each group also got its own cost target, because a sign-up from abroad costs far more than one from Lagos. Averaging the two would hide what is really happening.

What happened

Across the buyer campaigns in the screenshots, 784 Lagos buyers and 153 buyers abroad signed up.

Every Lagos campaign came in under the agreed target of ₦2,000 per sign-up, the cheapest at ₦362. Three of the four campaigns abroad came in under their target of ₦20,000, the cheapest at ₦6,091.

The proof

One campaign abroad was running over its target when these screenshots were taken. We rebuilt it with new ads instead of letting it keep spending. I left it in the screenshot, because hiding it would be dishonest.

How I work

Six steps. Every project.

01

Find the real worry

Not "who is the buyer" but "what stops them from buying". For buyers abroad, it is fear of being scammed from far away. For farmland, it is distrust of the whole idea. Everything else is built on this.

02

Make the offer answer it

If the worry is fraud, the offer leads with proof. Good writing can't save an offer aimed at the wrong worry.

03

Give one clear reason

One simple reason this works, clear enough that the buyer could explain it to their spouse over dinner.

04

Build the whole path

Ad, sign-up page, free guide, sales page, follow-up and tracking. A great ad sent to a weak page wastes money, and the page is usually the weak part.

05

Keep different buyers apart

Separate campaigns, targets and reports for each group. Averages hide what is really going on.

06

Report the number that matters

The cost of each serious lead, for each group. Not views. Not likes.
